# Empirical methods and interpretation

Run `python project_b/evidence/acquire.py`, then `python project_b/evidence/process.py`, then `python project_b/evidence/finalize_evidence.py` from the application root. The downloader uses public originals, hashes successful responses, and records failures without inventing substitutes. `process.py` uses standard-library ZIP/CSV parsing. Numerical values remain unrounded source decimals until calculations. Missing values remain blank. Duplicate tests use region, period and all relevant component dimensions. Derived year-over-year change is `100*(latest_index/prior_year_index-1)`.

The clean regional series retain December 2016 onward so that the base is common. Index *levels* should not be read as relative dollar costs between cities. Use percentage changes, or explicitly rebased trajectories, to compare price movements. A reduction in tax-exclusive NHPI is not proof that buyers kept all rebate value; without the no-rebate counterfactual, even a falling seller price could be higher than it otherwise would have been. The same applies to increases.

Fieldgate's scanned price sheet was rendered at 1.5 scale with PDFium, all three pages visually reviewed, and 48 offer rows manually transcribed into `raw/fieldgate_visual_transcription.csv`. `finalize_evidence.py` supplies source metadata and verifies 33 base-minus-advertised-rebate identities. Unpriced rebate fields on page 3 stay blank. Rows grouping multiple elevations remain grouped; they are not falsely represented as separate sold homes. Decor allowances are not converted into cash discounts. Any uncertain area/elevation layout is retained as printed, not inferred.

Published structural evidence is deliberately separate. [Paixao 2021](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/san2021-21.pdf) estimates supply responsiveness through sensitivity to regional house-price cycles, with employment, industrial structure, population and mortgage-rate controls. Toronto's published 0.89 and London's 19.6 describe historical structural responsiveness, not current developer stock or HST-specific supply curves. No numerical confidence intervals are transcribed because the cited passages do not give them. [Muellbauer, St-Amant and Williams 2015](https://www.oar-rao.bank-banque-canada.ca/record/6599/files/wp2015-40.pdf), printed page 25, reports an unrestricted inverse demand coefficient of 1.6 (t-statistic 2.5), described as a demand elasticity near -0.6. It covers Canada, 1982Q1–2015Q1, and long-run housing stock demand. The maintained model uses inverse elasticity 1.8. Combining this national parameter with a local supply coefficient would be a sensitivity exercise, not a measured local incidence result.

The course's competitive per-unit tax model can explain the inverse tax-cut question. It does not identify real rebate capture from these panels. Price and quantity are jointly determined; OLS of starts on prices would not recover a structural supply curve. Mortgage shocks may shift demand but also developers' financing costs, so their use as a demand-only instrument requires an exclusion argument and tests. No causal regression is promoted in this pilot.
