# Credit-card findings — November 10, 2026

The audit contains 24 product-family/jurisdiction records across 11 issuer brands, including six fully disclosed Tangerine product/jurisdiction combinations. It is not a count of 24 distinct cards, a market census, or a market-share sample. Rates for unmatched product disclosures remain blank.

RBC's January 2026 agreement §8 distinguishes Quebec highest-rate-first from proportional excess allocation elsewhere. Scotiabank's August 7, 2025 Quebec amendment supersedes its base agreement. National Bank's retrieved base agreement does not resolve the later Quebec change; that row is explicitly unresolved. Desjardins' outside-Quebec agreement also specifies descending rates at each payment stage. Thus Quebec is not the only setting with highest-first rules.

In the fixed, derived Tangerine outside-Quebec scenario (CAD11,000 initial debt; CAD600 monthly), proportional excess produces **CAD1,877.18** interest over **22 months**. Changing only excess allocation produces **CAD1,782.02** over **22 months**, a difference of **CAD95.16**. Starting categories and APRs are in the exported graphic and CSV. These are scenario results, not observed borrower outcomes or a policy impact estimate. A different balance mix, expiry date or payment changes the result.

Budget 2026 chapter 3 announces an intention to consult on requiring highest-first excess payments. The retrieved Bank Act section 627.35 still permits both methods. No enacted national switch is asserted. Read the source register for direct links and hashes.


# Housing findings

For an eligible $1,000,000 pre-HST builder purchase in the enhanced window, total relief is $130,000. Ordinary Ontario relief would already be $24,000, so the increase over ordinary rebates is $106,000. For a qualifying first-time buyer at that price, the pre-existing first-time federal/provincial package can already reach $130,000: the baseline matters. At $1,675,000, the enhanced package is $77,000; at $1,850,000 it returns to the ordinary $24,000. These are derived statutory amounts, not measured consumer benefit.

In the explicitly hypothetical market with Es=0.5, |Ed|=1, P0=$800,000 and Q0=100, a $20,000 tax cut lowers buyer price by $6,666.67 and raises seller net price by $13,333.33. Quantity rises by 0.8333 index units. These are chosen scenario values, not Ontario estimates.

The observed September 2026 house-and-land NHPI was 4.89% below its year-earlier level in Toronto, 3.97% lower in Hamilton, 1.94% lower in London and 1.00% higher in the Ontario part of Ottawa–Gatineau. The index excludes HST, condos and some market segments. These changes do not identify how prices would have moved without the rebate.

48 Fieldgate model/elevation offers were transcribed from an original price sheet, with 33 rows permitting exact advertised rebate arithmetic checks. One current Tamarack unit price was verified. There is no verified longitudinal unit-price panel; the original dated Tamarack historical sheet returned404 and its indexed figures remain unverified leads. No developer-capture percentage is reported.

**The newest structural products do not share one vintage.** Public national IO tables reach 2024, while the downloaded published multipliers end in 2022. Newer context reaches 2026-Q2 consumption, September trade, August real industry GDP and October employment. Those context series do not establish 2026 supply-chain relationships.

**The original retail question remains only partly answered.** No observed product-level margin and domestic/import decomposition was acquired. Therefore the pilot supports an industry-final-demand comparison and an explicitly hypothetical retail sensitivity calculator, not an empirically measured statement that a specific $100 Canadian versus imported consumer purchase adds a certain amount to GDP.
